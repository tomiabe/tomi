Me

My full name is Jesutomilayo Abe.

I was born in Lagos and come from a Yoruba family with roots in Osun State. I grew up in a home filled with love, faith, conversation, and the freedom to be curious. A lot of who I am today can be traced back to those early years.

I am a Christian, a husband, a Nigerian, and an African. I care deeply about the people around me, the places that have shaped me, and the ideas that make me stop and think.

This page is the more personal side of this site. A little about where I come from, what I believe, the people I love, and the things that have shaped the way I see the world.

Where I’m from

Lagos has always been an important part of my story.

I was born here, grew up here, left for a while, and eventually found my way back. Living in the UK gave me a different view of home. I noticed things I had taken for granted. I became more aware of how cities work, how institutions shape everyday life, how culture travels, and how much of what we know is carried quietly through families and communities.

I am interested in that kind of thing.

The stories behind places. The reasons people do what they do. The systems sitting underneath ordinary life. The things we inherit without noticing, and the things we choose to change.

Today, I live on the outskirts of Lagos with my wife, Toro.

Faith

My faith is central to how I live.

I am a Christian, and I see faith as more than something I believe on Sundays. It shapes how I think about work, responsibility, creativity, relationships, ambition, and the kind of person I want to become.

I believe creativity is a gift worth taking seriously. I believe good work matters, even when nobody is watching. I also believe curiosity is useful, but eventually you have to decide what to do with what you discover.

One verse I return to often is:

“Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord.”

Colossians 3:23

Faith gives me a place to stand while everything else keeps changing.

Family

I am married to Toro.

She is one of the people I admire most. Her faith, intelligence, warmth, and way of seeing the world have influenced me more than I can easily put into words.

Marriage has changed the way I think about many things. It has made me more attentive to another person's rhythms, hopes, fears, ambitions, and ordinary needs. It has also reminded me that a good life is built in the small things we do repeatedly.

Family matters to me. So do the friendships and relationships that have lasted through different seasons of life.

I am grateful for the people who know me beyond what I make or what I do.

What keeps me curious

I have always liked understanding how things work.

Sometimes that means taking apart an idea. Sometimes it means learning how a system works. Sometimes it is as simple as wondering why a street is designed the way it is, why a piece of software behaves a certain way, how a story came to be told, or why an old tradition has survived for generations.

I am drawn to art, design, technology, media, history, culture, sustainability, and faith. These interests do not always sit neatly together, and I am quite happy about that.

I like following a question until it leads somewhere unexpected.

A few things about me

I studied Computer Science at Covenant University and later studied Creative Technology at the University of the West of England.

I enjoy photography and tend to notice light, colour, architecture, streets, signs, people, and small details when I am out.

I like good conversations, especially the kind that continue in your head after they have ended.

I enjoy learning how people make things, whether that is a product, a business, a city, a piece of music, a publication, or a family tradition.

I am interested in Africa and particularly in the stories we tell about ourselves, the ones we inherit, and the ones we are still writing.

I spend a lot of time thinking about technology, but I am probably more interested in what technology does to people than in technology for its own sake.

I like making things.

I am still learning how to slow down.

Still becoming

I don't have a finished version of myself to present here.

I am still figuring things out. Some beliefs have become firmer with time. Others have changed. Some questions I have carried for years have found answers. New ones have taken their place.

That feels normal to me.

The work, the writing, the photographs, the projects, the conversations, the places I have lived, and the people I have loved have all become part of the same story.

This page is simply one way of telling some of it.
