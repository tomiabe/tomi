Journal

I write to think.

Some ideas start as questions. Some begin with something I have noticed, read, built, or disagreed with. Others take a while to become clear enough to write down.

The Journal is where I keep those things.

It brings together longer essays, independent publications, research, and shorter notes on culture, technology, design, faith, history, systems, work, and everyday life.

I do not write because I have everything figured out.

Usually, I am trying to figure something out.

Publications

The Limen

Connection, meaning, and the patterns beneath contemporary life.

The Limen is a publication about the things that sit between the obvious parts of life.

Technology and culture. Individual choices and wider systems. Belief and behaviour. The things we inherit and the things we decide to change.

It is a place for slower thinking and longer questions.

Undercurrent

Looking beneath Nigeria's present.

Nigeria is often discussed through what is happening now.

Undercurrent looks further back.

It explores the historical, cultural, political, and social forces that continue to shape the country and the way Nigerians understand themselves.

The starting point might be a current event, a familiar idea, a place, a person, or something I have encountered in everyday life.

The aim is to follow the thread underneath it.

Marketplace Logic

The systems behind digital marketplaces.

Marketplace Logic is an ongoing research and writing project with Alex Akinyeye.

We look at the decisions that make marketplaces work, from incentives and trust to supply, demand, discovery, pricing, network effects, and the products built around them.

The interface is only one part of the story.

We are interested in what happens underneath it.

Notes

Notes are shorter pieces of thinking that do not need the weight of a larger essay. They might be observations from a project, something I am trying to understand, a useful idea I came across, or a thought that has stayed with me.

Some become essays later.

Some are better left as notes.

Research

Writing and research overlap for me.

When I become interested in a subject, I tend to follow it further than the original question requires.

That has led me into questions about African history, sustainability, digital marketplaces, artificial intelligence, urban life, creative technology, media, design, and the systems behind everyday experiences.

Some of that research becomes a published piece.

Some becomes a project in the Studio.

Some simply changes how I think.

Writing in public

I have published across different platforms over the years, including Medium, Substack, and my own publications.

The platforms have changed.

The reason for writing has not.

Putting an idea into words forces me to slow down and decide what I actually think. It also makes the thinking available to someone else, who might disagree with it, add something I missed, or take the idea somewhere I had not considered.

That is one of the things I like about publishing.

What I am thinking about

The subjects change, but a few questions keep returning.

How does technology change the way we live?

What makes information useful?

How do systems shape everyday decisions?

What do we inherit from the people and places before us?

What does good work look like when nobody is watching?

How do we hold on to faith while remaining genuinely curious?

What should we build, and what should we leave alone?

I do not expect the Journal to answer all of these.

It is mostly a record of following them.
