Studio

Tomi Abe Studio

Tomi Abe Studio is my independent creative technology practice.

It is where I work with clients, build my own projects, test ideas, develop tools, and explore the questions that keep showing up in my work.

The practice has been running since 2013, alongside full time roles, research, consulting engagements, and independent projects.

The work moves between strategy, design, technology, research, communication, and making.

Sometimes there is a client problem to solve.

Sometimes there is simply something I want to understand.

Either way, I like getting close to the problem and making something real.

Polish

Self hosted design review

A local tool for reviewing interface code against practical standards for usability, accessibility, and visual craft.

I built Polish because I wanted a way to review real interfaces without sending the underlying code to another service. It turns design judgement into something more concrete by identifying issues, scoring them, and pointing back to the files that need attention.

Sound Atlas

A different way to explore music

An interactive experiment exploring music through geography.

Sound Atlas uses cities, playlists, and listening patterns to look at how music scenes develop, overlap, and travel. It started as a question about whether a map could become a better way to discover music rather than simply showing where music comes from.

Urban Climate Signals

Making urban climate data easier to see

A live exploration of city level heat and air pollution data.

The project brings environmental signals into one place so that changes in urban conditions can be seen more clearly over time. It is part data project, part visualisation experiment, and part investigation into how public information can become more useful.

Nigeria Embassy and Consular Finder

Finding diplomatic information without the digging

A searchable directory for diplomatic missions and visa processing information connected to Nigeria.

The idea came from a simple frustration: important information is often spread across different websites, presented inconsistently, and surprisingly difficult to find.

The project brings it together into a more useful interface.

NairaWatch

Following the other exchange rate

A small intelligence project tracking Nigeria's foreign exchange market.

NairaWatch brings together official exchange rate information and signals from the parallel market, using AI assisted interpretation to make the differences easier to follow.

It is an experiment in making a noisy information environment more understandable.

Smaller experiments

Not everything needs to become a full project.

Some ideas stay small. A prototype. A script. A visual study. A new publishing workflow. A tool built to solve one annoying problem. Something made over a weekend and abandoned a month later.

I keep these things too.

They are often where the more interesting ideas begin.

Signals

I use the Studio to pay attention to things that are changing.

AI and creative tools. New approaches to publishing. Interfaces. Data. Generative systems. Open source software. New ways of making and distributing things.

Sometimes that means building something.

Sometimes it means testing a tool properly rather than reading about it.

The practice

The Studio is intentionally small.

I do not want it to become a large agency with layers of process between the problem and the person doing the work.

When I work with a client, I usually stay close to the problem myself.

Frame the real problem

Before deciding what to make, I want to understand what is actually happening.

The brief is useful, but it is rarely the whole story.

Shape the system

The answer might be a product, brand, strategy, workflow, publication, data experience, or something else entirely.

The form follows the problem.

Make it real

I like ideas that survive contact with reality.

That means designing, prototyping, writing, building, testing, and adjusting until there is something useful to respond to.

Leave it stronger

The best work should leave something behind.

A clearer system. Better tools. Stronger internal capability. A useful product. A team that understands the problem better than when we started.

Ways to work together

There are a few ways I tend to work with people.

Strategic advisory

For teams working through a difficult decision, unclear direction, or a problem that needs an outside perspective.

Independent execution

For organisations that know what needs to happen and need someone who can take responsibility for making it happen.

Embedded collaboration

For teams that need senior creative and technical thinking inside the work for a period of time.

The shape can change depending on the problem.

What the Studio is for

I started Tomi Abe Studio because I wanted a place where different parts of my work could belong together.

Design and technology.

Research and making.

Client work and independent projects.

Practical problems and strange questions.

Some of the projects here will become useful products. Some will become writing. Some will disappear. Some will lead somewhere I cannot see yet.

That is part of the point.

I want the Studio to remain a place where I can follow an interesting question far enough to find out where it leads.
