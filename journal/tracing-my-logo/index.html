Tracing my logo

I have had this mark for more than ten years. It started while I was still at university, taking design work as a freelance designer in 2013. I graduated in computer science in 2015 and began to think about how I wanted my name to appear in the market. Freelancing turned into contracting, then consulting. The mark stayed with me through all of it.

The version I use now is a simpler take on one I made during my undergrad years. It is very easy to draw. People sometimes read it as a funnel or a lab beaker. It is really a T sitting with an A, the first letters of Tomi and Abe, folded into one shape. That was the whole idea.

I kept it black or white on purpose. It can sit on any surface or colour without me redrawing it.

I wanted to see what it would look like as a vector, and what I could do with it once it was one.

I wrote a small script to trace the PNG. It came back with two outlines: the outer shape and the triangular cut-out. In the browser, those outlines become a few hundred points that can be pushed around.

The question I kept coming back to was what should push them. I settled on my public GitHub activity. Each event picks a place on the outline, and the number of commits in a push sets how deep the dent goes. Older events fade. It only sees public events, so private work is invisible and anything older than about three months is gone.

The small logo in the site navigation uses only this ripple. When a new public event lands, one wave travels around it and settles. My first version drifted all the time, and that movement drowned out the shape. This way the mark stays still until there is something to notice.

The full experiment is on the logo page. You can compare the mark with the original, change how far it moves, or watch two other ways of animating it.

I don't know yet whether this stays. It began as a way to learn how the mark is put together. Now it moves when I do something public.
